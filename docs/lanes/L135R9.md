## What changed (`internal/guard/host_targets.go`)

1. **M1, subscripts.** `hostProvenReads` returns false when a segment holds an
   assignment word whose name has a subscript (`a[..]=`, `BASH_CMDS[..]=`,
   `BASH_ALIASES[..]=`, zsh `functions[..]=`, `commands[..]=`, `aliases[..]=`;
   `hostSegment.subscripted`). It also returns false for a `<<` heredoc with a `[`
   left open on its line (`hostInSubscript`): the shells read that `<<` as a shift in
   a subscript, so `echo $x[1<<'E']` and `echo x[1<<'E']` (a03, a06, a15, a16) are not
   trusted either. The floor then resolves the literal words, as main did.
2. **M2, option words the guard cannot decode.** `readCommand` calls a `find` or `rg`
   argument a runner, never a read, when `tok.ansi` is set (`-$'exec'`, `$'-exec'`,
   `-$"exec"`, `--$'pre'`, `$'--pre=tee'`) or when the word starts with `-` or an
   expansion and holds `$`, a backtick or `{` (`hostOpaqueOption`). Quotes and
   backslashes are removed by the tokenizer, so `-'exec'` and `-\exec` already matched.
   The `$`, backtick and `{` forms go beyond the kit rows and are the same hole:
   `x=-exec; find . $x tee lnk \;`, `find . $(echo -exec) tee lnk \;` and
   `find . {-exec,tee} lnk \;` were allowed by the lane, denied by main, and each
   writes under bash 3.2, bash 5.3.9 and zsh 5.9. A pattern such as `rg 'x$' file`
   (the `$` is not at the start) stays a read.
3. **M3, skipped comments.** The tokenizer emits a `hostSkipped` token where a `#`
   opens a comment; the segment records it (`skipped`) and `hostProvenReads` returns
   false. Covers the zsh glob-group lines d01-d04, where bash reads `#` after `|` or
   a blank as a comment and zsh reads it as a pattern byte.
4. `docs/lanes/L135R8.md` "Known gaps": the claim that main also allows a quoted `<<`
   shift outside `$[` and `((` is corrected; main denies a01-a21.
